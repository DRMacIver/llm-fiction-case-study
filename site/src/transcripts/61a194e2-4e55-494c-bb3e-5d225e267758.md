<li><strong>Paper</strong> — rag paper, water-powered mills, ancient craft. <span class="redacted">redacted sentence: unpublished prose</span> No wood pulp, so no throwaway printing. Combined with [Letters], this is the main reason the world reads later than it is, and it's free.</li>
<li><strong>Jars</strong> — glazed stoneware crocks from village and town potters, closed with a bladder or waxed cloth and string. No threads, no rubber, no tins. Glass exists and isn't for this. <strong>The keeping charm is why preservation technology never developed</strong> — Mattie's three-year damsons are a charmed crock, and preserved food is a household's magical competence on a shelf.</li>

<li><strong>Press</strong> — invented ~90 years ago, general use ~60–70, matching ch. 007's &quot;printing is new.&quot; Why never before: printing distributes impersonal knowledge and until Edwin there wasn't any. <span class="redacted">redacted sentence: unpublished prose</span> Manuscript still runs the registries, which is why &quot;you've put Fenholt in print&quot; bites.</li>
