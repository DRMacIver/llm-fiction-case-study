<blockquote><p><strong>David:</strong></p>

<div class="assistant-prose">
<p>Here's a proposed set. They're written to slot into <code>notes/voices.md</code> as a &quot;Dialogue&quot; section, with the same binding force as the narration rules.</p>
<p><strong>1. Every line has a want.</strong> Before a character speaks, the writer should be able to say what they're trying to get from the other person with this line (an answer, agreement, to close the subject, to make them feel something). A line whose only purpose is to be a good line is cut. Test: could you paraphrase the line as an instruction to the listener?</p>
<p><strong>2. Answer the question that was asked, then be clever if you must.</strong> If the question is practical (&quot;what's the difference?&quot;, &quot;did they want anything?&quot;), the first sentence of the reply answers it in usable terms. Epigrams may follow; they may not substitute. Exception: the speaker is <em>deliberately</em> not answering, in which case rule 4 applies.</p>
<p><strong>3. Narration bans apply inside quotation marks.</strong> Negative parallelism, triads with a swerve, aphorism budget (≤1 per scene, <em>total</em>, narration and dialogue combined), em-dash budget, house words. Dialogue has been the place these constructions went to survive.</p>
<p><strong>4. Obliqueness must be paid for on the page.</strong> If a line would leave its listener confused, one of three things happens next: the listener says so and the speaker clarifies; the listener visibly gets it and the reader has been given enough to get it too; or the speaker is being evasive and the evasion is itself the point and is treated as such. What may not happen: a scene break, a topic change, or a stage direction (&quot;she shifted the chain&quot;) so the line goes unanswered.</p>
<p><strong>5. No echoes.</strong> A character does not repeat the previous speaker's sentence verbatim or near-verbatim as their reply. Budget: at most one deliberate echo per <em>arc</em>, and it must be doing something a plain reply can't (mockery, shock, ritual). &quot;The rings.&quot; / &quot;The rings.&quot; is banned outright.</p>
