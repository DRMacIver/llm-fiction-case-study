<h2>What's actually shared</h2>
<p>Four constructions do the blending:</p>
<ol>
<li>
<p><strong>The self-justifying imperative.</strong> Short command + <code>because</code>-clause aimed at an adversarial future reader. <span class="redacted">redacted</span> <span class="redacted">redacted sentence: unpublished prose</span> <span class="redacted">redacted</span> can take apart in one reading.&quot;* Same sentence, same ethic, same tactical grounding of honesty. Cover the tags and you cannot tell.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><span class="redacted">redacted sentence: unpublished prose</span> That is Edwin's virtue — the record as conscience — handed wholesale to <span class="redacted">redacted</span>.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Numbers as the payload of the line.</strong> Both open on a bare figure and unpack: <em>&quot;Eight,&quot; said <span class="redacted">redacted</span>. &quot;Four each.&quot;</em> / <em>&quot;Four sites,&quot; said Edwin. &quot;Three soils.&quot;</em></p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Closed utterances.</strong> This is the abruptness. Nearly every line either character speaks is a complete, pre-defended ruling. Neither ever picks up the other's word, leaves a sentence unfinished, or asks a question they don't already have the answer to. Two people who each finish their own argument produce adjacent monologues, not an exchange — so the scene reads as a series of hard landings regardless of sentence length. The problem isn't clipped prose; it's <strong>no uptake</strong>.</p>
</li>
</ol>
<h2>The fix, and it's already in the book</h2>
<p>029:111 is the seed and everything else contradicts it:</p>
<p><span class="redacted">redacted sentence: unpublished prose</span></p>
<p><span class="redacted">redacted</span>'s inherited ethic is <strong>oral, public, witnessed</strong>. Edwin's is <strong>written, measured, defensible on paper by a stranger</strong>. That's a real and generative difference — and outside that one speech she's the book-and-record character, which is his. Four rules follow:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>He records; she says it in front of people.</strong> Move <span class="redacted">redacted</span>'s accountability from the ledger to the witness. <span class="redacted">redacted sentence: unpublished prose</span> Then their disagreement about <em>which</em> one counts becomes live material instead of a shared temperament.</li>
<li>Authority by transmission vs. <span class="redacted">redacted sentence: unpublished prose</span> Edwin fundamentally isn't — his whole project is measuring the received wisdom. Currently she gets this register twice (018:137, 019:177) and it's her best material. Make it her default reach where he reaches for a figure he took himself.</li>
<li><strong>Split the numbers.</strong> <span class="redacted">redacted</span> counts <strong>people, days, miles, stitches</strong> — things you get by walking and headcount. Edwin measures <strong>rates, yards per day, assay figures</strong> — things you get from an instrument and a column. Hard rule: she never quotes an assay figure, he never rosters.</li>
<li><strong>Strip her <code>because</code> clauses.</strong> Her characteristic unit should be two flat sentences whose relation is left for the listener to make — <em>&quot;It's a hand. </ul>
<p>And one scene-level rule for the abruptness: in any Edwin/<span class="redacted">redacted</span> exchange, at least one party must fail to complete their case — a repeated word, a real question, a sentence that stops. Right now neither ever does.</p>
