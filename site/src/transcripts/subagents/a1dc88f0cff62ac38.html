## WHAT WOULD FIX IT

I do not think this is repairable by patching; objections 1, 2, 3 and 5 are load-bearing and independent. But for the next attempt, the constraints this exercise establishes:

1. **The bottom chamber is dry and the second is wet.** Whatever fills a Deep does not fill it from the bottom, and any water model must explain a dry lowest floor beneath a flooded upper one — perched water, an inflow at the second chamber's level, or water that is not obeying gravity at all.
2. **Water is audibly running, at volume, in a hill redacted: character says has none.** That is a bigger anomaly than the tide mark, it predates the strain (ch. 014, from outside, before redacted: event), and no candidate should leave it as a corroborating footnote.

4. **The air did not change between the 14th and the 21st** while the water dropped eighteen inches. Air and water level are not coupled the way a manometer couples them.
5. **Whatever it is must be able to exist under the sea**, in unlimited supply, unbuilt and unbuildable, or `dungeons.md`'s sea section has to be reopened first, on the record.
6. **The energy budget is the real question.** ~700 animals per fifteen months out of a dry hole with a steppable trickle. Any answer routed through ordinary trophic ecology has to show its arithmetic, and this project will hold it to real numbers.
7. **Warrens are rabbits.** The word is glossed, on-page, in an author's note. It is not available as a pun.
8. **Do not resolve to drainage.** That mechanism is taken. Whatever a Deep is, its cause and its dating instrument must come from a different drawer than the soil answer's — and ideally its discoverer should not be Edwin with a level.
