3. Recurrences → candidate tier=regex rules

Constructions confirmed 2+ times across files. Regexes are deliberately over-broad recall filters for a judge stage, not verdicts.

(a) negative-parallelism-split — highest value; 4 hits, currently invisible to lint

Scenes 8, 25, 26 (×3 in one scene), 30. The banned not-X-but-Y with the "but" swapped for a full stop or semicolon; contractions dodge the existing regex. It lands on thesis lines ("It isn't lost time. It's the first task"; "So the secret isn't the sentence… It's the doing").

(?i)\b(it|that|this|the \w+)\s*(?:'s not|s not|\s+is not|\s+isn't|\s+wasn't|\s+was not)\b[^.;!?]{0,120}[.;]\s+(?:It|That|This|The \w+)\s*(?:'s|s\b|\s+is|\s+was)\b

Plus the semicolon-only variant: (?i)\bnot\s+(?:the|a|an)\s+\w+[^.;!?]{0,80};\s+(?:plain|just|only|simply|the|a|an)\b

(b) amount-of-it — 3 hits (scenes 6, 20, 22)

"the whole of his trade", "four generations of it", "six days of being right… any of it". DRMacIver's standing note: "the whole of it continues to be a weird recurring phrase". Existing regex misses the the whole of his X form.

(?i)\b(?:the whole|the most|the rest|the least|the best|the worst|all|some|any|much|none|a (?:great )?deal|\w+ (?:days|weeks|months|years|generations|mornings))\s+of\s+(?:it|them|his|her|their|the)\b

(c) dialect-past-tense-come — 2 hits (scenes 9, 12), both under dialect-grammar

The bare-come-for-came form recurs specifically. Cheap and near-zero FP if anchored to a subject.

(?i)\b(?:it|he|she|they|girl|man|word|post|letter|news|that|which)\s+come\s+(?:up|from|in|down|round|over|back|out|to)\b

(d) trailing-which-verdict — 3 hits (scenes 4, 33, 34) across causal-which-budget and verdict-clause-evaluative

The narrator's trailing appraisal clause. Two rules are catching one habit; consider merging, or at least sharing a regex prefilter.

(?i),\s+which\s+(?:was|is|were|seemed|reads?|counts?|amounts?)\s+(?:fair|true|right|wrong|the (?:best|worst|point|whole)|about|more or less|not)\b

And the ranked-verdict variant: (?i)\b(?:her|his|their)\s+\w+(?:st|th|nd|rd)\s+\w+\s+of\s+the\s+(?:morning|day|afternoon|week)\s+and\s+the\s+(?:best|worst|sharpest|first)\b

(e) the-way-simile clustering — 2 confirmed clusters (scenes 6, 30) plus scene 37's type-of-person-clause

The existing budgets fire, but the adjacency is the real tell (two horse similes in one paragraph; two "the way X does Y" a paragraph apart). Suggest a proximity check rather than a new pattern: flag any two matches of \bthe way (?:a|an|the|you|somebody|people|\w+) \w+ within 400 characters, regardless of the per-1,000-word rate.

(f) Maxim-capped speech — 14 confirmed instances across 11 scenes

Too semantic for regex, but the cadence is stable enough for a recall prefilter: a sentence that is the last of a quoted turn, contains no proper noun and no first/second-person pronoun, and opens with a generic plural or "A man/woman who…". Worth building as a small script over dialogue-final sentences rather than a rules.json regex — the judge stage already handles these well; the problem is the hunters' hit rate, not the judging.

sense — 20 FPs (31% of all FPs; 6 confirmed)

The rule is functioning as the hunters' catch-all: anything a reader found slow gets filed here. Note the judge also had to re-file two confirmed sense hits under other rules (scene 19 → ambiguous-word; scene 13's "third thing" → not this rule at all).

Proposed narrowing. Split the rule in two, as the feedback-does-this-sentence-make-sense memo already implies: sense-factual (the sentence asserts something contradicted elsewhere in the same scene, or physically false — scenes 20, 34, note 33) and sense-parse (the sentence is grammatical but garden-paths — note 31, scene 16). Then add to sense-factual:

counter_example: A sentence that merely requires a second pass to parse, or whose referent is recoverable from the preceding two sentences, is not a sense error. Slowness is not nonsense. If the sentence is true and the reader gets there, do not file it. Do not file vagueness, undefined-on-first-use terms, or round approximations under this rule.

Twelve of the 20 FPs are "needed a re-read" or "the figure is loose"; that clause alone should clear most.

implication-stopped-short — 4 FPs (2 confirmed)

Hunters are firing on any withheld information, including legitimate close-third restraint.

counter_example: In Edwin-POV narration, declining to report another character's thought is not an unpaid implication — it is the viewpoint working. This rule covers only a line whose point is insinuated and which no adjacent sentence or immediately following event cashes out. A dry countable observation left unglossed (a tally, a refusal already stated above it) is within voice.

maxim-capped-speech — 4 FPs (14 confirmed)

High yield, but hunters flag every sententious-sounding line regardless of budget. The rule is a budget rule and needs to be enforced as one.

counter_example: A single maxim-shaped line in a scene is within budget and is not a finding on its own; file only when the scene carries two or more, and always nominate the keeper. A line attributed to a character's stated source (a book she reads, another person's saying) characterises rather than coins, and does not count against the budget.

generalising-clause-budget — 3 FPs (1 confirmed)

Overlaps heavily with aphorism-budget and maxim-capped-speech; the same clause gets filed under two or three of them, and hunters guess.

Proposed narrowing: restrict to trailing subordinate clauses in narration that generalise outward from the named particular ("because a village does not keep a thing like that from a mother"). A free-standing narrator sentence belongs to aphorism-budget; a line inside quotation marks belongs to maxim-capped-speech. State that precedence order in all three rules — it is the single biggest source of cross-filing in this run.

tech-level-anachronism — 3 FPs (1 confirmed)

counter_example: Pre-industrial parishes have iron, glass, paper, ink, wheels, clocks in a church tower, and post. Flag only objects whose manufacture requires industrial process or whose social form is post-1700 (a kitchen range, a match, a percentage, a factory-made tool). When in doubt the rule is flag-and-query at low confidence, not a confident finding.
