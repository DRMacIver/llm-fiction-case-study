## Two lines a scene

**47.** The device a second round would be about is **Mattie's mouth**: the scene's whole engine is her reading dated bands aloud, which is Edwin's and redacted: character's currency, and the "what week the barley thinks it is" line is that leak surfacing as an epigram. To make it work, keep every date but hang each on an act she did (the hand in the crop, the hay she cut, the alehouse keeper's field) and let Edwin do the arithmetic out loud; to remove it, give the week-counts to Tess reading the board back.

**48.** The device is **the eleventh-of-Cornmonth paragraph**: as written the narration argues the coincidence away in three sentences and then confirms the significance through Mattie, which is louder than saying nothing. To make it work, cut to the two of them looking and neither writing; to remove it, let the date land with no paragraph at all and trust the reader who has counted from the fifteenth.

**49.** The device is **the dirt geometry as an explanation**: the three fixed marks and four fitted middles are followable, but the error argument that justifies them contains an impossible number (a hundred and sixty yards across a sixty-yard ring), and one wrong figure there costs the reader the whole construction. To make it work, restate the conditioning in terms of how far round each arc the three points spread; to remove it, drop the error paragraph and let *noise* stand on Peri's page unargued.

**50.** The device is **the prediction lecture**: the content is right and welcome, but it is delivered *to* the woman who has just paid for it and who invented the second control, so it reads as teaching aimed at the wrong person. To make it work, address it to the book and to Peri with Mattie's leave — one clause does it; to remove it, let Peri's "which way would you be wrong?" be the only frame the prediction gets.
