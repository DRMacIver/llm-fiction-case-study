Only one construction clears the bar: contamination-overlap, confirmed 4× in one file (26 runs). It is already tooled by tools/overlap_check.py and should be promoted from a judged lens to a hard tier=regex-class gate — it needs no judgement at all, and its judged FP rate is 0%.

WORD_RE = re.compile(r"[a-z0-9']+")
NGRAM_N = 8

def ngrams(text: str, n: int = NGRAM_N):
    words = WORD_RE.findall(text.lower())
    for i in range(len(words) - n + 1):
        yield " ".join(words[i:i + n])

def contamination_runs(scene_text: str, corpus_ngrams: frozenset[str]) -> list[str]:
    return [g for g in ngrams(scene_text) if g in corpus_ngrams]

5. Did haiku-level hunting work?

Mixed, and the split is informative. Of 19 candidates, judges confirmed 8 and rejected 9 — a bare-majority rejection rate, which on its own would look like a failing hunter. But the rejections are concentrated almost entirely in four rules that fail for one shared reason: the scanner reads a quote without its neighbouring sentence. canon-scale-contradiction cannot see that the sentence before names a habit rather than an age; cause-left-out and `implication-stopped-short cannot see that the next sentence supplies exactly what they claim is missing. Where the pattern is genuinely local and surface-visible — verbatim overlap, a five-item list, a portable epigram — the hunter was right every time it fired, and a judge even added a finding the scanner missed. So the architecture works: cheap wide hunting plus expensive narrow judging caught real contamination and a real budget overspend across two scenes, at a cost of nine cheap rejections. The fix is not a better hunter model but a one-sentence context window on the candidates it emits, plus the counter-examples listed in §3. With those, the projected FP rate on this sample falls from 47% to roughly 10%, which is comfortably inside what an opus judge should be asked to filter.
