## Findings

**Part 1 — does this sentence make sense?**

1. "a causeway built for one opinion at a time" — Part 1, *a word doing a job it cannot do*. "Opinion" has just been used figuratively of the road; reused here it means nothing literal (a causeway is built for one *cart* at a time), and the reader stalls on it. Replace: "along a causeway built for one cart at a time".

2. "at twenty past six in the evening by his status, three hours older than the plan" — Part 1, *check the clock*. Twelve miles at the four hours the arithmetic allows puts arrival three hours after the plan only if they left the Sluice at about twenty past eleven, which contradicts "while the morning was still cool" and a scene that has them walking most of a day. Either name a late departure or make the figure fit; simplest: "**six hours older than the plan**" (drover an hour, the windpump keeper and his three questions, the gates, the bridge, teaching stretches — six is what the paragraph actually spends).

## Devices a second round would be about

The one device likeliest to draw a whole-scene comment is **the street's not-naming**: the boy's cut-off "r—" plus "there's a word they're all not saying" plus Edwin's "we'll likely find out" is three separate signals that the scene is withholding, and the reader already knows the word is *rings* from scene 28 — so the withholding buys nothing it has not already spent. To make it work, drop one of the three signals (the "r—" is the one to cut: the hand on the shoulder does the whole job) and let Peri's line be about the hand, not about the word.

The second is **Edwin's sheet-refusal speech**, which is currently the scene's argument, its aphorism, its contrastive pair and its teaching paragraph in one block. With findings 20–21 applied it stands as a reason given on the page; if a comment comes back at it a second time, the repair is structural — let Peri take it apart in two exchanges rather than receiving it entire — and not another rewording of the last sentence.
