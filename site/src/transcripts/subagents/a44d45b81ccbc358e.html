2. NARRATION: Close third omniscient with hard, unmarked head-hops between scenes and even within scenes/sentences (e.g., switching from Cheis's tactical POV to Ulbert's backstory mid-paragraph). Scene breaks (***) cut between the three converging threads almost cinematically, escalating tension via cross-cutting. 6. EXEMPLAR PASSAGES:

- "Velinaer himself, who naturally survived almost completely unscathed, struggled to the top of a tremendous pile of debris and stared out at the wreckage and ruin stretching nearly to the horizon in every direction. In his terminal, a small alert appeared, informing him that his power reserves had dipped to 3.19 ulasovs." — the flat status-readout after mass devastation is the book's signature LitRPG-deadpan move, letting the UI element do the emotional work of horror-via-understatement.
- "He was alone -- completely, irreversibly, and utterly alone, and also naked since his clothes hadn't survived the devastation -- and the only person who was going to praise him was himself." — the comma splice tacking bathetic "also naked" onto genuine existential grief is the whole book's tonal method in miniature.
- "But, at the end of the day, the facts were inescapable: when all one has is a hammer, one tends to perceive the universe primarily as an environment which allows one to interact with nails. Peace never stood a chance." — a chapter-closing aphorism that's simultaneously a joke and real characterization/theme statement, very Pratchett.
- "Cheis, who was standing back a bit and observing their foe... Wow, look at that instruction density. Was that a recursively encrypted command exchange stack? She added more wards to herself, feeling underdressed." — magic-as-programming register used for both worldbuilding and a self-deprecating joke ("feeling underdressed") in one beat.
- Dialogue: "'You killed a rakshasi using tricks you cobbled together from your training item?' Cheis was impressed, and that did not happen often. 'You've got potential, kid. Maybe I'll make a good apprentice out of you yet.'" — mentor-voice warmth delivered dryly, no sentimentality, immediately followed by practical danger-warning, which is the book's warmth-with-teeth mode.

7. WEAKNESSES: The tech/programming jargon is laid on extremely thick in fight scenes ("floating-point underflow," "recursively-encrypted command exchange stack," "malformed packet into the kid's data processing pipeline") to the point of overwhelming legibility of the actual physical action — a reader can lose track of who's winning a fight under the joke-density and metaphor-stacking. Battle sequences run long and blow-by-blow, and the constant tonal deflation of every death (even innocent bystanders/civilians) risks numbing stakes if overused — the "Apecis... had not so much perished as questionably existed in the first place" line is funny but also a slightly glib dismissal of mass death that a more restrained author might cut. Numbers/statistics-as-jokes (12,848%, 5.617e9%, 3.19 ulasovs) are a good bit but used repeatedly enough to become a slightly predictable tic. The father-son Galar/Linduin reunion is compressed almost too fast into banter, undercutting potential emotional payoff.

8. Y/N"), and Velinaer's death-magic explicitly coded as hacking/networking/sysadmin work (teledemonics as literal IT infrastructure, "root password," "line-of-sight sanitization," malformed-packet attacks). This is used consistently as a character-voice/worldbuilding device rather than mechanical stat-porn — it characterizes Velinaer as an anxious IT professional out of his depth and gives Cheis's power a hard, funny, legible scaling logic, and it's deployed for humor (self-aware boss-fight staging: "The heroes had to fight their way through minions... Look at me, I'm evil. Come and get me.") more than for crunchy progression-tracking.
