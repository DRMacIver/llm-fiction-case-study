Part 1 — does this sentence make sense

1. "Peri sat up in the window seat of her room that night with the notebook, the mist standing off the back of the inn the way the landlord had said it did, and made the day's entries."
Part 1, viewpoint knowledge (2026-08-30): the scene is Edwin's viewpoint and he is not in her room at night; and the landlord says nothing about mist anywhere on the page, so the back-reference has no antecedent.
Replacement — move the whole beat to breakfast, where he is: "At breakfast Peri turned the notebook round on the table and put her finger on a heading, because she was pleased with it."

2. "She did not show him the entries, then or later, but at breakfast she showed him the heading of one... Under it she had written: first magic since leaving..."
Part 1, a line that contradicts itself / viewpoint: the narration says he was shown the heading only, and then quotes verbatim the sentences under the heading. He cannot have the wording.
Replacement — she shows him the page, not the heading: "...put her finger on a heading, because she was pleased with it. The drain had gone under LESSONS, not under SIGNS. Under the heading she had written, and left where he could read it: ..."

3. "first magic since leaving"
Part 1, check the clock: they left the village that same morning (scene 25), so "since leaving" is a span of about nine hours, and the entry is written as though a stretch of time had passed. Also it sits oddly against scene 26, where she says she has watched him do more magic before breakfast than she has done since the spring.
Replacement: "first magic on the road, and it was about a drain."

4. "The seven miles from the bridge had taken the whole afternoon, the causeway being a road that let you walk fast and the fen either side being a thing Peri stopped for, twice, to write about."
Part 1, an inference where Y does not follow from X: both "being" clauses are offered as reasons the seven miles were slow, and the first is a reason they should have been quick.
Replacement: "The seven miles from the bridge had taken the whole afternoon, though the causeway was a road that let you walk fast; the fen either side was a thing Peri stopped for, twice, to write about."

5. "Why sixpence? Not why so little. I worked that out on the winch: nobody haggles with a favour, they just feel bad, and feeling bad curdles. Why anything?"
Part 1, a reply/argument that contradicts itself: the winch insight she reports — that a favour leaves people feeling bad — is already the answer to "why anything?", so she asks a question she has just answered. (Also Part 2: "feeling bad curdles" is a figure with nothing under it.)
Replacement: "Why sixpence? Not why so little. I worked that out at home, on the Corrys' well-winch: a big price makes a man argue about it and a small one makes him pay it and be glad. Why anything at all?"

6. "I worked that out on the winch"
VOICE §2, callback phrases never carry meaning on their own — a reader who has not read scene 13 does not know what the winch was. The finding is elaborate; the elaboration is in the replacement at (5): name the Corrys' well-winch and what happened.

7. "The landlord went up the stairs at a pace that suggested the mason was going to hear about the fall of his drain before he heard the end of his own supper."
Part 1, pronoun with no clear antecedent: "he heard the end of his own supper" reads first as the landlord, and the sentence means the mason.
Replacement: "...before the mason had got to the end of his own supper."

8. "'That's sixpence,' Edwin called after him, 'when the drain's proved it.'"
Part 1, reference with no clear antecedent / compressed to code: "it" is the reading, three paragraphs back. Elaborate.
Replacement: "'That's sixpence,' Edwin called after him, 'when they've had it up and it's proved me right.'"

9. "Since the spring they go off their water in three days at that end"
Part 1, a word doing a job it cannot do: "go off their water" reads as refusing to feed, and the meaning is that they sicken and die; it is one of the two places the eel tank has confused a reader before.
Replacement: "Since the spring they sicken in three days at that end"

10. "Has anything been done to the yard since the spring?"
Part 1, does the justification distinguish / Part 4: he has not been down the cellar or smelled anything yet, and he names the yard — the answer to the puzzle — as his first question. It pre-empts the reading the scene then performs.
Replacement: "Has anything been dug or re-laid since the spring? Anywhere about the house."

11. "standing at the north wall with the lamp… He knelt at the wall's foot, worked the old trowel into the joint" / later "Peri, holding the lamp" / "He took the lamp off her."
Part 1, check the dependents: Edwin has the lamp, then kneels and works two-handed, then Peri has it with no handover on the page.
