**1.** "it had now happened thirteen times in his presence, twelve of them today."

**3.** "eleven stations" (in the report to Ferrin).
— Part 1, *check every count / dependents of every edit*. Eleven is the number of **castings**, three of which were outside the field. Told to Ferrin as stations it is a different and false claim.
Replacement: "eight stations inside and three on ground he trusted".

**4.** "At one o'clock he cut the centre sample … and the day's fieldwork was done." — placed *after* "The rest of the afternoon was hands and spade."
— Part 1, *check the clock*. One o'clock cannot follow the rest of the afternoon; and the six revealings, eleven castings, the digging and the mole runs cannot all fit before it.
Replacement: "At six he cut the centre sample…" — and then the following "it was past six in the evening now" at the gate needs to become "past seven", with "thirteen hours" adjusted to fourteen.

**5.** "cast them the way he had cast them for a hundred and eighty years" against "Most of this work, for three hundred years, has been casting the same six spells".
— Part 1, *a line that contradicts itself*. Two different spans for the same activity, twenty paragraphs apart, and nothing in between distinguishes them.
Replacement: make the first span belong to the *set in this order* and say so: "cast them the way he had cast them since he settled on the order, which was a hundred and eighty years ago" — or simply make both three hundred.

**6.** "the null case of the quickening assay was his own design, built with deliberate overlap … and it was never meant to be able to happen"
— Part 1, *the wrong noun for a real thing*. A null case is an outcome, not a thing that is designed or built with overlap; the assay is. As written the sentence says he designed the null.
Replacement: "because the quickening assay was his own design, built with deliberate overlap so that no error and nobody selling him a doctored sample could slip a dead reading past the full set, and a nought in every kind was never meant to be able to happen".

**7.** "so that no error and no liar could slip a dead reading past the full set"
— Part 1, *an explanation stops halfway*. "No liar" arrives with no referent — nobody in the scene is lying to an instrument, and the reader cannot construct who would.
Replacement: fold into the above — "nobody selling him a doctored sample" — or cut "and no liar".

**8.** "the worms held the same line the census held: none inside, and no dead ones, and the ordinary crowd two spits beyond the edge."
— Part 1, *a word doing a job it cannot do*. A spit is a spade's depth, a vertical measure; "two spits beyond the edge" uses it as horizontal distance.
Replacement: "and the ordinary crowd in the first spit a yard the other side of the edge".

**9.** "The moles knew in Seedmonth," said Edwin.
— Part 1 (2026-08-30 additions), *viewpoint knowledge*. He has just found the runs; the scene gives him no basis for dating them to Seedmonth, and it is the load-bearing claim of the beat.

**10.** "That's what it did in the box," said Peri.
— Part 1, *a pronoun or reference with no clear antecedent*; VOICE §2 "callback phrases never carry meaning on their own". A cold reader cannot say what box, whose, or what "it" did in it — and Edwin's verbatim echo, which is the beat's best move, inherits the fog.
Replacement (elaborate, per the standing caution): "That's what it did in the box," said Peri. "The soil you cut at the edge and carried home. It came back nought in every kind, and you said the box must have been wrong." Then Edwin's echo lands.

### Part 2 — the gesture at meaning

**14.** "You don't reach for the fine instrument until the coarse ones have cleared the ground it stands on."
— Part 2, *a figure of speech must compare two things the reader can hold*. The figure is mixed: coarse instruments do not clear ground, and a fine instrument does not stand on ground. It is also the scene's third portable maxim (see 17).
Replacement, plain mechanism, which is what the paragraph already half-says: "Coarse to fine. Every one of the first five can produce a strange census on its own, so you settle them first, and then a strange census means something."

**15.** "and where the instrument's name would have gone on any other line, he wrote the man's name instead."
— Part 2, *significance claimed rather than cashed*, and VOICE §2's *resolving button*: the closing act is arranged into a symmetry rather than reported. The content underneath it is excellent and must stay — Edwin has entered Ferrin as an instrument — so this is a plaining, not a cut.
Replacement: "…wrote the priest's words down at the foot of the day's findings, entire, and in the column where he put down which instrument had given him the reading, he wrote *Ferrin*."

**16.** "which Edwin translated as *crowded*"
— Part 6, *italics for a quoted phrase inside narration read oddly*.
Replacement: "which Edwin wrote down as crowded".
