**What the Seal does.** [Seal of the Loom] does not assign a destiny. It opens an empty field in a person's fate and ties the world's small causality to it. **What fills it in.** The field is written by the System off the bound person's own life, and it can only be written when that life contains a **candidate commitment**: one specific thing the person has taken on *by their own motion*, in the world, whose completion horizon **exceeds the span the System projects them to have.** In plain terms: the Destiny fills in with *the thing you have started that you will not live to finish.* No candidate, no fill-in. The System is not choosing a fate; it is running a query it already has the data for (it counts a life broadly — ch. 028's clerk's-work class level — and the aging race between levels and years is exactly a span estimate).

**The wording of the fill-in is always the same shape.** Because the work is by construction unfinishable inside the span, the System never writes the work. It writes the only discharge condition an unfinishable thing has: put it in hands that outlive you. Edwin's box, when it comes, reads:

> [Destiny: the teaching of successors.]
> [Terms: until fulfilled.]

Flat, administrative, an anticlimax after five books of dread, and **the plural is load-bearing and nobody remarks on it for a book.**

**Whether the holder can influence it.** Completely, and only in one way: by *taking something on*. You cannot write the field, argue with it, negotiate it, or ask what it will say. You can commit to a thing too big for your years, and then there is something to write. Refusing to commit keeps it blank forever. The binder cannot influence it at all — Peri gets direction-and-distance on the bound man (ch. 006) and nothing else; she cannot read the content unless he chooses to display it (display is the recipient's choice, canon). Her death does not end it. Nothing ends it but fulfilment.

**What "fulfilled" tests.** Not completion of the work. The test is **the work's independence of the man**: does the thing now have people, a method, and a record such that it continues at its ordinary rate with him removed? The System tests it the way it tests every tier-up — retrospectively, noticing that you have already become the thing, announcing at a stupid moment while you are doing something else. `[redacted: mechanic.]` arrives hours late, in a field, and the knot behind the breastbone goes. **What refusal costs.** In System terms: *nothing at all.* No penalty, no wasting, no tightening knot, no clock. The System does not punish anywhere in this world and does not start here. The only System-side consequence is that `until fulfilled` becomes, functionally, *never*, and the improbability engine keeps running for the rest of your life — deniable line by line, escalating only in frequency and pointedness (established canon).

The cost is human, and it is this: **the engine has no separate supply of luck. It is made of other people's.** It reallocates — attention, timing, arrival, whose letter goes astray, which road is out, which job lands in front of whom — always *toward* the destiny, never punitively, never breaking anything. A man who refuses for thirty years is not struck down; he acquires a very faint statistical wake of other people's rearranged small luck, which no magistrate would look at twice, and which he is the one man on the continent professionally equipped to read, because reading evidence that only says anything in aggregate is his whole trade (ch. 003, verbatim).
