### 1. Where she is from, and what the household was

She is a redacted: character: wool warehouses, merchant wealth **one generation deep** (`peri.md`, ruled 2026-08-28). Her grandfather carried; her father buys, stores and forwards. Prosperous, not gentry, not old, and conscious of both.

The important fact about the household is not its money. **It is a house whose entire livelihood is being right about next year.** A wool house buys forward: it commits money to a clip before it is shorn, on a judgement about weather, fleece weight, and what the fair will pay. A firm one generation deep survives on somebody in it guessing well. So Peri grew up at a dinner table where adults argued about what was going to happen, on evidence, with money on it, and where a wrong call in Milkmonth was still being discussed at Yule.

And the method was **not writable, not taught, and not opened to her.** The house ran on the buyers' *eye* — an unexaminable competence, transmitted by standing next to a man for fifteen years, spoken of the way people speak of a gift. Ask how it is done and you get: *you'll get an eye for it*, or *because I've been forty years at it*, or *run along*.

There is no cruelty here and no wall. Everyone was fond of her. Her brother is older and gets the firm; her expected life was two or three more years of being a schooled daughter and then a marriage into an older, better-established house — which is precisely what merchant wealth one generation deep buys with its daughters, and nobody thought of it as a cage because it wasn't one. redacted sentence: unpublished prose

### 2. What she was doing before, and what she expected

Being finished, and being a nuisance. From about nine she asked the buyers *how do you know?* and would not stop at the first answer, or the third. She was not apprenticed, not favoured, not secretly running the ledgers. She was the daughter who followed the wool men round the warehouse floor asking questions they could not answer, for the better part of ten years, and was tolerated with affection and never once given a method.

**She expected the marriage.** Not with dread — she had no theory that anything was wrong. What she had was a specific, chronic, unspectacular frustration: *there is a room where things are actually decided and nobody will tell me what happens in it.*

**She did not want to save the world before the Choosing.** This is load-bearing and is the answer's discipline against the theme constraint. She wanted an answer to one small question about how knowledge works.
