## SCENE 39 — "Read it as six"

### Part 1

**"registers coming from fifty miles off that I haven't seen"** — Part 1, *check every count*. Contradicted eleven lines later by his own post arithmetic in the same scene: "Four days to Wystow if the roads are kind, six if they're not." A carrier does not take four to six days over fifty miles, and Appleford is "forty miles away" in this same speech, so fifty reads as nearer than Sorrel. Replacement: "registers coming from a hundred miles off that I haven't seen".

**"I'll reckon on an answer the twelfth day and be pleasantly surprised or not surprised."** — Part 1, *check every inference*. The figures he has just given are 4+4 at best, 6+6 at worst, *plus* "something at their end takes what it takes". Twelve is therefore the bad roads with zero days at Wystow — the one combination he has just said he cannot count on. Replacement: "I'll reckon on an answer in a fortnight and be pleasantly surprised or not surprised." (Peri's next line then becomes "A fortnight.")

**"Peri, who was mending the day's pages across the table"** — Part 1, *a word doing a job it cannot do*. "Mending" pages means repairing torn paper; what she is doing is correcting or fair-copying the day's entries. Replacement: "who was making up the day's pages across the table".

**"this is the other half of a handover, and you saw the first half in the tower kitchen, the night the lists were made"** — Part 1, *a reference with no clear antecedent*; VOICE §2 *callback phrases never carry meaning on their own*. A reader who has not read scene 19 gets nothing from "the first half" or "the night the lists were made", and the speech then builds on it. Elaborate: "…you saw the first half in the tower kitchen the night before I left, when she and I cut the vale's work into two lists and I gave her the longer one and did not ask to see it again." (Also worth checking against 19 that the room is the kitchen and not the study — 05 puts their list business in the study.)

**"a week like this one"** — Part 1, *check the clock*. Day 11; he crossed the Ashway on Day 7 and reached Fenholt on Day 9. Five days, not a week. Replacement: "after the week he'd had" → "after the days he'd had", or name it: "in five days like these".

**"you saw … the night the lists were made" / "I have broken exactly one written date in three hundred years"** — the second is fine against canon (110 years ago, a coast turn, eleven parishes); no change. Flagged only because the brief asked the count be checked.

### Part 2

**Narrator close: "on the grounds that other people's letters, like other people's ground, want leave before digging."** — Part 2, *the gesture at meaning*, and it over-frames the beat exactly as the brief suspects. Canon requires the no-letters-home thread to stay small, instant and unremarked (`peri.md`, redacted: character ruling); a closing figure that compares her privacy to a field being dug tells the reader the beat is significant and supplies a reading she has not earned. It is also the scene's only narrator aphorism spent on a decoration. Not a cut of a reason — a cut of a gloss. Replacement, keeping a plain reason: "and Edwin put the wax away and did not ask, because it was hers to send or not."

**"a finding with one name on it can be buried with the name"** (inside the Wystow letter) — Part 2 / Edwin 6c. Fifth portable closer in the scene (see the budget finding below). Plain version: "One name is easy to lose. Four are not."

### Part 5 — the aphorism budget (the scene's largest finding)

Edwin gets **one composed teaching paragraph, one aphorism, and one contrastive pair per scene** (`edwin.md` 6c, 13; `voices.md`). This scene runs **four composed paragraphs and seven portable closers**. Survivors and replacements:

**KEEP (the aphorism): "a written date gets altered while it's still alive."** It is the scene's spine, it is his canon rule, and it is the lesson he has explicitly framed as one.

**KEEP (the contrastive pair): "You hand work to somebody entitled to alter it, or you haven't handed it over at all."** — but it currently *closes* the paragraph, and 6c forbids the last sentence being the most portable one. Move it to the head of the paragraph ("Sorrel doesn't need my reasons. You hand work to somebody entitled to alter it, or you haven't handed it over at all. She'll build better ones…") and end the paragraph on the fact: "…she'd be making them in Fallowmonth weather with Fallowmonth facts, and I'd be making them tonight with none."

**CUT/REPLACE — "You've just made them your clerk at a distance"** — Part 5, *redundancy*: it restates the pair immediately after it. Cut the sentence.

**CUT/REPLACE — "It's by altering your dates early, when the world alters, and never once pretending the old date is still alive when you know it's dead."** — Part 5, *redundancy* (it says the aphorism again, in the same alive/dead figure) plus a second portable closer. Replacement, plain and ending on fact: "You alter the date the day the world alters, and you say in the letter what altered it. That's the whole of it."

**CUT/REPLACE — "A man who signs alone once is brave. A man who's allowed to keep signing alone is being spent by people who won't put their own names at risk"** — Part 5 / `edwin.md` 4: anaphora, two clauses of equal length, and the most portable line in the speech; all three are the named defect. Replacement: "Signing alone once is brave. Being left to do it a second time means nobody above him is willing to be wrong in public, and I've watched colleges do that to their best clerks for three centuries."

**KEEP but plainen — "The letters work. Slowly, which is the same speed as fields."** The callback to scene 13 is right and it answers her question; the trailing clause is a third aphorism. Replacement: "The letters work," said Edwin. "They work slowly. Nothing in that envelope will be less true in twelve days, and if it would be, it isn't ready to send."

**"I'd sooner lose a hand than do that to her."** — Part 4 / `edwin.md` 4, *at a peak he is plainer and factual about himself*. Hyperbole is not in his register anywhere in canon, and it lands oddly on a man whose arm is about to matter. Replacement: "and I have never once done that to her, and I'm not going to start at forty miles."
