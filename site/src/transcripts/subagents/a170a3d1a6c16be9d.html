Findings — scene 31, "The pantry door"

Part 1 — does this sentence make sense?

"and watched some of the fortnight go out of Mattie Ordish's shoulders when he did." — Part 1, pronoun/reference with no antecedent (and viewpoint knowledge). "The fortnight" is not established until line 41, twenty lines later, and Edwin has not yet been told about it; in paragraph 1 it refers to nothing the reader or the viewpoint has. It also pre-empts the later line, which is the scene's best inference. Replace: "and watched her shoulders come down a little when he did."

"served by a woman of about fifty with the reddened hands of somebody who works in water" — Part 1, the wrong noun for a real thing / reference. She was named and met on the page one scene earlier; introducing her as "a woman of about fifty" and only naming her three clauses later reads as a fresh introduction of a stranger. Replace: "served by Mattie Ordish, whose hands were the reddened hands of somebody who works in water, and eaten under her eye to see that it was eaten." (This also fixes "under her watching", which is a verbal noun doing an adjective's job.)

"The pantry door, on its inside face, carried the whole of it." — Part 1 (reference: the whole of what?) and Part 2 (banned family: "'the whole of it' continues to be a weird recurring phrase that you should avoid"). Replace: "The pantry door, on its inside face, carried the record."

redacted sentence: unpublished prose Replace: "and that was all she said about the fortnight."

"Between the chain figures sat the other kind of entry: … the day the hay went in; … the day the sheep came off." — Part 1, check every count and every fact. The hay went in in Weedmonth (the month of the second chain figure) and the sheep came off in Milkmonth, and the loft has held nine weeks, which puts today well past Weedmonth; the sheep entry is not between the two chain figures. Replace: "In among them sat the other kind of entry:".

redacted sentence: unpublished prose The door shows two chain figures (late Seedmonth, Weedmonth) with nine weeks since; monthly is what he is about to institute, not what she has done. redacted sentence: unpublished prose

redacted sentence: unpublished prose Replace: "It's a record, and it's the only one I know of, from its first season, and it's dated."

"and I'd like you to put your mark against my copy" — Part 3, literacy. This world writes everything down and she wrote the door; a mark is what someone who cannot write leaves. Replace: "and I'd like your name against my copy".

"the gate and the thorn stump and the water trough all marked" vs "The gatepost and the stump." — Part 1, the wrong noun. Canon's line runs SW gatepost to thorn stump. Replace: "the gatepost and the thorn stump and the water trough all marked".

redacted sentence: unpublished prose Replace: "the same rotation twenty years of her own, and her father's before that."

"And the hay off the ring came in green in Weedmonth," said Edwin, reading the entry — Part 1, viewpoint knowledge: the entry as described says only "the day the hay went in". Green is not on the door. Fix at source: make the door entry read "the day the hay went in green".

Part 2 — the gesture at meaning

"small and quiet as furniture" — a figure whose two halves the reader cannot hold: furniture is not small and is not quiet, it is silent, and "small as furniture" is not true of any furniture. Replace: "who had been writing in the corner of the pantry all this while and had not said a word,".

"and her face closed like the door of a house" — a weak simile competing with the actual door the whole scene is about; a face closing like a door is the dead version and "of a house" does not rescue it. "The questions turned the evening" — the flat version is empty: the questions changed the evening in what direction? The clause after it is a real reason and should stay.
