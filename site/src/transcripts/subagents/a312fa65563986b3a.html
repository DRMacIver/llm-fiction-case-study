The three candidates, and the bet each makes about why a reader turns the page.

The Rising Water bets on imaged consequence: you can picture the world in which this thing wins. "A yard a day is a fifth of a mile in a year."

The Open Wager bets that mystery is competing hypotheses a reader can score. "That is the list. There is nothing else on it. ... redacted sentence: unpublished prose Four of them. Curved."

The Call-Out bets on jeopardy in a body, in someone's yard, now. "Say it in yards. Not for the book. For me, now, at my own gate."

Verdict: the Call-Out for the sentences, the Open Wager for the architecture, one instrument from the Rising Water and nothing else.

The Call-Out won two scenes of three, and it won the two that the book is mostly made of: ordinary procedure and redacted. Its fuel is also the only renewable one. Dread depletes; a per-chapter symptom paragraph is invisible by chapter forty. Live hypotheses deplete too, and this particular mystery has few. But a named person wanting something done with money on it is inexhaustible, and the story is already structured as two people going farm to farm at other people's request. It is also the least of a departure from what you have.

It failed the discovery scene, though, and it failed exactly as its own spec predicted: the yard got so interesting that the actual finding, the four circles whose centres march, never made it into the scene. That is why the Open Wager goes in as plot rules rather than prose. redacted sentence: unpublished prose Those belong in arc plans, not sentences. Twice in its own samples the Open Wager had to write a paragraph whose only job was to keep score, and both times it broke its own bans to do it. The scoreboard cannot be carried by the narrator.

The single most important amendment: openings are entrances, not minutes. No scene may open on a sentence stating its own outcome, duration, or verdict. Forty-six percent of your scenes currently date-stamp themselves and ten percent more announce how long they will take. All three voices invented this rule independently, all nine samples have a better first sentence than their source, and it is the mirror of endings-are-cuts, which is what pushed the weight to the front in the first place.
