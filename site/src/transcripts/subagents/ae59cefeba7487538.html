WEAKNESSES: The prose sometimes over-explains its own jokes and world mechanics — the "explanatory pamphlet" gag about the lich system, the "ulasovs" energy-unit riff, and long process-narration of Velinaer's tinkering can run long and read as padding rather than momentum; a few paragraphs (e.g., the extended suspension-fluid engineering passage) indulge in worldbuilding detail past the point where the joke lands. Some sentences are baggy/run-on with piled-up subordinate clauses that dilute punchlines rather than sharpen them. The dialogue occasionally tips into having characters explain the theme to each other quite directly (Cheis's "cost of magic," Galar's "division of labor" essay-paragraph) — useful for a serial audience tracking rules but risks didacticism an author aiming for tighter Pratchett-style economy should watch. The sexual-tension/romance beats (Pellamin/Cheis/Umbria) lean on fairly familiar love-triangle mechanics without much freshness compared to the sharper comic and magic-system material.
