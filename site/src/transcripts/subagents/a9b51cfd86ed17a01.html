1. "It was Widow Sepp who said it, at the biscuit tin."
Part 1 — a pronoun or reference has no clear antecedent. The scene opens on a cleft whose it points at nothing; the reader is told a thing was said before anything has been said, and the previous scene ends on Edwin, not on any "it". (The groundwork's "who put it together" was queried for a different reason — she already knew — so this is not a line to rescue by re-wording.)
Replacement: "Widow Sepp waited for the biscuit tin. She had been watching Peri sideways all morning, and she spoke to the garden at large, the way she said most things."

2. "at the biscuit tin" / "the eleven women with trowels in their laps"
Part 1 — check the clock. The tin goes round on the half hour (ch. 002; minor.md, Dob's "biscuit tin at half past"), i.e. 10:30. Scene 07 puts Peri's questions after "the first half hour", with the compost cantrip last and "Can you actually fight?" over the compost bed — so this exchange sits at the end of a class that Sorrel then says has run to ten past eleven. The tin cannot be where the class ends up. The trowels in laps compound it: the class has just finished casting.
Replacement: move the beat off the tin — "Widow Sepp waited until the trowels were down" — or, if the tin is wanted, have it come round late for once and say so in the same clause ("The tin went round late, the class having overrun").

3. "and he watched her do the sum the long way, out loud, in the wrong order, which was, he was learning, how she did sums."
Part 2 — the gesture at meaning, and the unwanted explanation; also Part 1 — check every inference. "He was learning" claims a pattern off one prior instance — scene 02's "You'd need — you taught the teachers" — on Day 2 of knowing her.
Replacement: cut the whole clause. "She looked at the heap, and at the eleven women with trowels in their laps, and at him."

Replacement: "Between you you've made it run to ten past."
