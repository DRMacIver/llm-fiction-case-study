# Canon and claim levels

Canon, in this project, means the directory of files recording what's been established about the world, the characters, and the timeline, kept separate from the plans and separate from the prose itself. What makes it more than a wiki is that every entry carries an explicit claim level, so that a fact's status is visible without having to guess how firmly it's meant.

Established means the fact is on the page in an accepted chapter, and the entry cites where. Working means it's binding until someone overrules it but hasn't actually appeared in the story yet, which covers design decisions and anything the model proposed and David accepted. Open questions, as of a triage pass partway through the project, are no longer allowed to sit undifferentiated. Every open question in canon must be answered, ruled on with a decision, or explicitly deferred with a named trigger, something specific that will force the question to be resolved when it happens. A deferral with no trigger counts as a defect in the canon file rather than a legitimate state.

## Why the undifferentiated state got closed off

This tightening has a specific cause. Earlier in the project, a mystery was introduced on the page without anyone having actually decided what its answer was. Nothing was wrong with introducing it unresolved, that's normal craft, but the project let it sit as a bare open question with no plan for resolving it, and chapters kept accumulating evidence around it. By the time anyone checked, there was a real risk that no answer would fit everything already written. The rule that followed forbids that specific state: an open question now has to be either resolved, ruled as a working decision, or deferred with a stated trigger, no exceptions.

A related instrument, kept separate from ordinary canon, is a directory of sealed answers: for load-bearing mysteries, the actual answer is written down before or at the moment the mystery appears on the page, but it's written only by a subagent and never shown to David unless he explicitly asks. This exists so that consistency checking can verify every clue is coherent with a real answer, without spoiling the answer for the person who's going to read the finished book like everyone else. A session asking, deliberately without wanting the content, whether a coherent explanation for one such mystery actually existed shows the boundary in practice: [2026-08-27, checking a sealed answer exists](../transcripts/10c1dd77-c31a-4be9-a7b6-23651a4792b4.md).

## Where facts come from and how they grow

Canon isn't written speculatively ahead of the story. A skill called consistency-backfill runs after a chapter is drafted and checked, reading what the chapter implies, not just what it states outright, and updating canon files to match: recording a stated fact as Established, an implied one as Working, and, where canon was previously silent on something the chapter now depends on, deciding the smallest binding commitment and recording that as Working too, preferring to under-specify rather than over-commit. Where a chapter conflicts with existing canon, the fix is proposed but never applied silently: it goes back to David as a flagged decision, quoted, with the reason and the cheaper of the two possible fixes.

The counterpart skill, consistency-check, runs the other direction: given a draft, it checks every claim against the canon files in priority order, core facts and continuity first, with theme weighted softer than the rest, and reports contradictions, or the weaker category the project calls drift, where a claim is technically compatible with canon but strains it. Neither skill is allowed to weaken or delete an existing canon entry to make a draft fit. If canon and an earlier accepted chapter turn out to disagree, canon wins and the earlier chapter is flagged for revision, rather than the model quietly editing canon to fit what was already written.
