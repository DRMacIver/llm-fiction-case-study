# 2026-08-27

**Four rounds of blind dialogue testing, a decision to treat all of Book 1 as disposable groundwork, and a full canon audit across every chapter.**

The day opened with David asking, without being told any plot details himself, whether Claude's own notes held together as a coherent explanation for the story's central mystery. That kind of sealed, spoiler-bearing answer file, readable by Claude but withheld from ordinary drafting context, became a recurring device from here on, checked and rebuilt across several adversarial review passes through the day.

The larger effort was dialogue. David had noticed the characters' conversations felt artificial: too many clever dangling lines, characters answering questions with non-answers, and everyone slipping into the same aphoristic register regardless of who was speaking. Claude ran blind tests, stripping character names from lines of dialogue and asking whether a reader could still tell who was speaking. The first round scored badly. Over three more rounds, with adjustments each time (shorter lines, an explicit fifteen-word cap in testing, forcing coverage of certain props and details each character would notice), attribution scores climbed sharply, one late test scoring 27 out of 27 lines correctly assigned. Voice rules for each major character were written up as explicit, countable constraints rather than vague description.

Partway through, David gave process feedback that reshaped how the project ran from here: stop reaching for subagents for every small task, and instead reserve interactive back-and-forth for the main session while pushing bulk mechanical work to cheaper models running in the background. That became house policy for the rest of the project.

Late in the day came a decision with real consequences: all thirty-one chapters drafted so far, the whole of book one, were reclassified as groundwork for a rewrite from scratch rather than as a finished draft. That made a batch of earlier decisions about locked voice choices moot, since nothing published yet actually depended on them. A five-domain canon audit followed, checking theme, worldbuilding, magic and system rules, and timeline arithmetic against all thirty-one chapters, correcting a handful of consistency errors it found along the way.
