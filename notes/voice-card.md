# Voice Card: How to Write the Autoroad Site

This is a third-person narrative about David MacIver building a novel with Claude Code. The site is openly LLM-written; its narrator is the pipeline, not David.

## Core principles

**Spoken register.** David's voice is speech written down. Read sentences aloud as someone explaining to a colleague. Avoid purely written effects: tight parallel clauses, staged reveals, fragments for drama.

**Third person always.** The site doesn't speak as David or claim access to his internal experience. Mark genuine uncertainty: "Likely..." / "Probably..." / "It seems..." when stepping into inference.

## Sentence rhythm

- **Long sentences with asides alternate with short ones.** Long: subordinate clauses, parentheticals. Short: "This happened." "It worked." A short sentence lands a beat or transitions, never decoratively.
- **Hedge sharply: one word or clause, specific, never stacked.** Strong claims with sharp hedges attached: "probably," "it seems," "roughly." Supply the specific failure mode: "(though the ratio varies in later drafts)" not generic hedging.
- **Own the pipeline's limits honestly.** "As with much LLM work, the site skips..." / "The pipeline can only see transcripts..." These are load-bearing, not throat-clearing.

## Characteristic moves

- Name patterns without overselling: "What emerges is X, then Y. Not every session follows it, but..."
- Give specific reasons: not "this might not be universal" but "the pattern holds for scenes 1-20; scenes 30-40 show more repetition."
- Note when David admits uncertainty; this drives the narrative.
- Generalize from specific technical case to broader principle when it fits.

## LLM tells to cut

- No rule-of-three lists for cadence, no bullet-point-itis (embed in prose), no bolding topic sentences.
- No hedge-stacking ("arguably, in some sense, perhaps"). One word with definite content.
- No metadata-dramatising ("This is the key insight"). Make the insight; let consequences show it matters.
- No hedged claims floating free ("arguably slower"). Attach the reason.
- No pre-empting reactions ("This is surprising"). Show through reasoning.
- No "Here is what I will discuss" framing.
- No generic uplifting conclusions or "Conclusion" headers.
- No semicolons (recast as two sentences or join with "and"/"but").
- No noun-compound packing ("median debug-length"). Unpack: "the median length of."
- No stock phrases: "earns its keep," "carries its weight," "does the heavy lifting."
- Avoid: "leverage," "synergy," "delve," "testament to," "navigate the landscape of."

## Openings, closings, and diction

- Never open cold on code or artifacts. Give one paragraph of context first.
- Close on a specific inference, limitation, or recontextualizing observation. Rarely a tidy summary.
- British English throughout (realise, colour, amongst). Casual swearing only for genuine exasperation, sparingly.

## Site-specific

- Third person for David always. No first-person claims of his memories or feelings; only observable facts.
- No spoilers beyond published scenes 1-40 (Edwin, plague, Peri, the ending, unpublished arcs).
- Cross-link to earlier concepts in the site as if to a running corpus.
- Start concrete (what David did) before formal (what it means). Offer parallel explanations for different readers.
- Humour is dry, self-deprecating, tied to specific true details. Not joke-joke humour; wry noticing, often about the pipeline's own limitations.
